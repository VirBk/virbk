  Item 2 — a quoted YAML scalar does not carry its quotes into the value.
  `parseTrapsYaml` reads `id: "T71"` and `kind: 'machine'` with the quote characters still
  attached, so the id, the kind and the title are wrong whenever a child quotes them, which
  YAML permits everywhere. Strip ONE matching pair of surrounding single or double quotes
  from a scalar after collapsing it, and only a matching pair: an apostrophe inside a rule
  is not a quote character and must survive.
  MUTATION THAT MUST TURN IT RED: remove the unquoting. A fixture whose rows quote id, kind
  and title must fail the self-test naming the value that carried its quotes.

  Item 3 — an explicit refusal is honoured, not silently absorbed.
  `trapsCandidate` maps kind through `row.kind === "machine" ? "machine" : "portable"`, so a
  child writing `kind: reject` has its do-not-absorb signal read as portable and its row
  emitted as a candidate intake. That inverts the child's own instruction. A row whose kind
  is `reject` (after item 2's unquoting, case-insensitive) is HELD and never emitted, and is
  counted and named by id with its reason, exactly as a row naming no check already is. This
  is the existing two-outcome design, emitted with its check or held with the reason; do not
  invent a third outcome and do not add a contract field — intake.mjs holds its own field
  list and refuses an unknown key.
  MUTATION THAT MUST TURN IT RED: map `reject` back to portable. The self-test must fail and
  name the property: a row that refused absorption was emitted.
